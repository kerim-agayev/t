### Wheel
- `Wheel_0.mp3`  In 1974, the psychologists Amos Tversky and Daniel Kahneman described an experiment much like this.
- `Wheel_1.mp3`  People watched a wheel of fortune spin. It was marked 0 to 100, but rigged to stop only at 10 or 65.
- `Wheel_2.mp3`  They said whether the true answer was higher or lower, then moved up or down from that number.
- `Wheel_3.mp3` [curious] Different groups got different starting numbers. The question stayed the same.
- `Wheel_4.mp3`  Starting from 10, the median answer, the one in the middle, was 25 percent.
- `Wheel_5.mp3`  Starting from 65, it was 45 percent.
- `Wheel_6.mp3` [surprised] A wheel knows nothing about the United Nations. Yet the two groups' middle answers were 20 points apart.

### Careless
- `Careless_0.mp3`  Were they just being careless? The authors report that paying for accuracy did not reduce the effect.
- `Careless_1.mp3` [thoughtful] Later work adds a catch: incentives can help when people know which way to adjust.
- `Careless_2.mp3` [playfully] So here is a second test from the 1974 paper, and this one you can try yourself.

### Multiply
- `Multiply_0.mp3` [excited] You have five seconds. Don't work it all out. Just estimate this.
- `Multiply_1.mp3`  Got a number? In the study, high school students had five seconds to estimate a product like this.
- `Multiply_2.mp3`  But one group saw it starting from 8. Another group saw it starting from 1.
- `Multiply_3.mp3`  The group that saw 8 first gave a median guess of 2,250.
- `Multiply_4.mp3`  The group that saw 1 first gave 512.
- `Multiply_5.mp3` [amazed] Same numbers, same product. And the real answer is 40,320.
- `Multiply_6.mp3`  The authors' explanation: people may work out the first few steps, then adjust upward, but not far enough.

### House
- `House_0.mp3`  Still, an expert, you might think, would see straight through it.
- `House_1.mp3`  In a 1987 study, Gregory Northcraft and Margaret Neale gave estate agents a 10-page booklet on a house.
- `House_2.mp3`  It held the facts needed to price it, plus a listing price: above the appraisal for some, below for others.
- `House_3.mp3`  The agents' estimates moved toward that listing price. So did the estimates of non-experts.
- `House_4.mp3` [thoughtful] To be fair, a listing price can carry real information. So here is a harder case.

### Judges
- `Judges_0.mp3`  Trial judges, with more than 15 years of experience on average, were asked to sentence a hypothetical criminal case.
- `Judges_1.mp3`  They considered a sentencing demand that openly came from a computer science student playing prosecutor.
- `Judges_2.mp3` [serious] In the researchers' account, judges shown a demand of 34 months gave almost 8 months more than those shown 12.
- `Judges_3.mp3` [surprised] In later research, junior lawyers threw secretly loaded dice to set the demand. It still pulled their sentences.

### Why
- `Why_0.mp3` [curious] So why does a number pull, even when you know where it came from?
- `Why_1.mp3`  One leading explanation: to compare, your mind tests the number, and facts that fit it come forward.
- `Why_2.mp3`  In one study, people judged whether the average German car costs more or less than 40,000 marks, or 20,000.
- `Why_3.mp3`  The researchers reported that after the high number, people spotted words like Mercedes and BMW faster.
- `Why_4.mp3` [serious] And after the low one, words like VW. But a 2019 team ran new versions and did not find this word effect.
- `Why_5.mp3`  Anchoring still appeared, and their word test may be too weak to settle why. So treat the explanation as open.
- `Why_6.mp3` [playfully] Some starting points you pick yourself. To guess when vodka freezes, you may start at water's 0 degrees Celsius.
- `Why_7.mp3`  Then you move downward. With starting points like these, research suggests we tend to stop too soon.

### Limits
- `Limits_0.mp3` [serious] Now the part that is easy to leave out. Not every number you happen to see will grab you.
- `Limits_1.mp3`  In a 2020 paper, David Shanks and colleagues found that incidental numbers had no reliable effect on price estimates.
- `Limits_2.mp3`  But the classic setup, compare first and then estimate, has held up well.
- `Limits_3.mp3`  Many Labs, a project with 36 samples and 6,344 participants, gave a high or low hint first. Anchoring appeared.

### Defense
- `Defense_0.mp3` [thoughtful] So can you simply watch out for it? A warning may not be enough.
- `Defense_1.mp3`  In one study, students were warned, some even told which way the pull would go. The pull did not go away.
- `Defense_2.mp3`  Something else did help. Car experts who listed reasons against a suggested price were pulled less.
- `Defense_3.mp3` [warmly] So here is a habit to try. Treat it as my suggestion, not a proven cure.
- `Defense_4.mp3`  Make your own guess before you hear theirs. Then ask: why might their number be wrong?
